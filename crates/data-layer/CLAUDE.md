# crates/data-layer

The data layer over `crates/pdn-store`, our iroh-docs variant: the `SyncNode` stack assembly, the access book that classifies every session (reads filtered on egress, writes judged by the fork's `validate_entry` hook per ADR-0008 — both installed at spawn), the metadata stores, the retraction tracker, and the protocol-agnostic ceremony slot. One node hosts any number of identities, each with a half of the node it owns: its own docs engine, replica store, registry, access book and author (ADR-0013). Every session names the identity whose replica it addresses and the identity its caller acts for, and is judged by the named identity's records alone. Enforcement arms per identity by registration (`host_identity` / `host_connection`) and per replica by the grantee import, and a PMS or connection metadata store starts its sync at that arming rather than at its import, since a session started before the book can judge it is refused — `host_connection` also points the own half at the devices the peer half is dialed at, so whichever side arms second reaches the first over both; a data replica no registration covers is refused rather than served, while a PMS and a connection metadata store keep the ticket bound Invariants 1 and 3 give them. Ownership runs from `SyncNode` down: every engine gets only the sending end of the node's co-located request channel, and the node's task serving it holds the identity map weakly (`serve_co_located`), so a node dropped without `shutdown` still releases its engines, stores and directory. The ceremony slot — spawn-time registration of externally supplied protocols plus a narrow dial handle — is consumed by pdn-node's two handlers, pairing per ADR-0011 and linking per ADR-0012, and stays protocol-agnostic here because the ceremonies' semantics live in pdn-node.

Stores: the device-replicated `PrivateMetadataStore` (the PMS: the one store of an identity's own state — its devices, confirmed and pending, the typed tickets to its other stores, its connections records, its retraction markers, its identity key pair — plus the namespace accessor and the bounded catch-up wait the linking ceremony stands on) and the cross-identity `ConnectionMetadataStore` (one replica per connection direction, issuer-written / counterparty-read per Invariant 3, carrying grants keyed by data-store issuer and the issuer's published device set). `forget_namespace` is the rollback counterpart of `import_namespace` — it unregisters the issuer, not just drops the replica — and `undo_import_namespace` is that same act under the name the import's caller undoes by, doing nothing after an import onto the replica its issuer already resolved to, which bound nothing.

The identity key pair and everything derived from it or signed by it live in `identity_key.rs`, their context strings side by side, since those strings are what keeps one kind of signature by the key from passing as another. A pod's two stores register by pod id in the identity's registry, beside its data namespaces: `create_pod` creates both or neither, `import_pod` takes their write tickets — `import_pod_with` beside them tickets to the same stores other identities minted, each node dialed as the identity whose ticket names it — and `forget_pod` at a departure drops the record store and keeps the membership store, out of its swarm, as the pod's tombstone. Every import path refuses a ticket naming a namespace the identity holds in another role, so one replica is never a pod's store and something else at once. Both stores' sync starts once they are registered, and the periodic pass leaves them to a pass of their own at `SpawnOptions::pod_reconcile_interval`: each run, every change to the membership store — a local write within the write, an arriving entry or payload at once, anything else after `SpawnOptions::pod_change_settle` — and every device the identity's PMS newly lists, which each store then dials as the identity (`watch_own_devices`), derives both stores' contacts from the membership view — every current member's devices, dialed as that member, and the identity's own devices by its PMS, dialed as the identity, this device as itself left out — replacing them whole, and stating them to the engine, unless the replica's membership view lists nobody yet — and each run reconciles each store with at most 5 of them and the engine's recorded peers, drawn afresh, through the fork's `Doc::sync_with_peers`, since `start_sync` dials every recorded peer. A session on either store is served to a device of the member the caller names — a sibling by the identity's own PMS, another member by the device statements the identity's membership view counts, waiting briefly for the payloads of the caller's own entries when they are on their way — and to nobody else but a former member's device, served the membership store over its departure's past alone, both ways (`pod/past.rs` computes the past from the event set); a device whose own identity departed holds its sessions with member devices to its past too, taking its own later events beside it so a new join reaches it, and its tombstone is reconciled with the identity's own devices alone once a session with a member's device has gone through. The record store's every dial follows an exchange of the membership store with the same counterpart, over the network through the fork's `order_after` and inside the process by the node itself, and the pass over two co-located members takes a pod as one pair of both stores. Their key layout is in `pod/keys.rs`, and `pod/membership_view.rs` builds the membership view from a membership store's entries, as one device holds them: the pod's members and a verdict per entry, reading no store: a pure function whose cost grows with the entries it reads. `pod/record_view.rs` reads a record store's entries by that membership view, each entry judged on its own against it, so a read of one record queries that record's prefix alone and loads only its payloads, while every read builds the membership view over the whole membership store again; the same view numbers a device's next operation on a mergeable-document from the entries its replica holds under the device's author, so the count continues across a restart. Every derivation of a pod's contacts also reports, on the channel `take_pod_notices` hands the runtime, while the record store is still held, a hosted identity's chain ending in a departure and a member's device list missing this device with the identity's author, and `flush_pod` reconciles the stores an identity holds of a pod with at most 5 devices of its other members, its `PodFlush` waiting for one session with them per store; the PMS records a departure with `tombstone_pod` and answers the highest record of a pod with `pod_record`. A pod's sync starts with the contacts its membership already derives, beside each ticket contact whose node they leave out — a ticket names every node as the identity that minted it, so a reopened replica would dial a member's device as itself — both stores' read before either starts, since the membership store's first session derives them again from a membership view that may list no device of the inviter yet, and a derived contact keeps the addresses a ticket gave its node, since without address lookup a node id alone is undialable to a device that never met it, a restarted one included; `open_pod_tombstone` reopens a departed pod's membership store alone, out of its swarm.

The `DataLayer` trait in `layer.rs` is declared and implemented by nothing; the runtime drives `SyncNode` directly.

Blob collection runs over the node's one blob store at `SpawnOptions::blob_collection_interval`, its single protect callback answering with the payloads every hosted identity's replica store references, and on a storage directory starting only at the host's `start_blob_collection` — pdn-node calls it once recovery has hosted its identities again — so a start removes nothing of an identity recovery has yet to reach. The collection's task holds the store until it stops, so a node dropped without `shutdown` stops its blob store in its drop.

Storage is a required choice of every spawn (`SpawnOptions::storage`): memory by name — the workspace's suites, via their spawn helpers — or a directory by name, with no default. A configured directory holds everything a node needs to be itself:

```
<directory>/
  node.key                  the endpoint's secret key: the node id survives a restart
  lock                      the running node's exclusive hold; a second node on the directory is refused as DirectoryHeld
  peers                     the address book: the last addresses of every peer a replica syncs with or names, handed to the endpoint at the next start
  blobs/                    payload bytes, one store for the node
  identities/<identity>/    one identity's half of the node
    docs.redb               its replica store
    default-author          the one author its writes carry
    pms                     its hosting record: its PMS's namespace, written at the commit point of a create or link
```

`provision_identity` is a no-op for an identity already hosted when the call starts, and two concurrent calls for one identity are not supported: pdn-node never makes them — `create` mints a fresh id, `link` runs under the runtime's state lock after its already-hosted check, recovery provisions one identity at a time — and a caller that provisions in parallel has to hold an in-progress set first. Each hosted identity's stores write with that identity's one author (`SyncNode::default_author`, persisted with its replicas), and what one identity's replica store may hold is a share of a node budget, cut as that store opens from the identities `identities/` then records as hosted, this one included — so what an unfinished create or link left takes no share, and no host states a count (`SyncNode::replica_cache_share_bytes`, `replica_cache_budget_exceeded`); `PrivateMetadataStore::open` reopens a PMS replica the node already holds — recovery's constructor, beside `create` and `import` — and answers `Ok(None)` for a namespace the store does not hold, so a caller acting on a durable record tells an absent replica from a store that failed to answer.

Reachability is the other spawn-time choice (`SpawnOptions::connectivity`), and the constructor names it rather than the caller assembling it. `Connectivity::Direct` binds nothing beyond the socket — a peer is reached at an address the endpoint publishes about itself or not at all — and it is what `memory` and `on_directory` give the suites and the container stand, whose peers already hold an address for each other. `Connectivity::Relays` adds the relay servers iroh's own `default_relay_mode` names, so a peer behind a NAT is reachable and the relay URL travelling in every ticket and ceremony payload keeps the node dialable after its network changes. `Connectivity::RelaysAndAddressLookup` adds publishing and resolving under the node id, which is what `for_product` gives a node shipped to a device the project does not control: without it a contact carrying a node id alone — a sibling read out of a device record, a peer whose ticket addresses have gone stale — is not dialable at all. The three map onto iroh as `Minimal` with the relay mode set explicitly, twice, and then the `N0` preset whole.

Scenario tests in `tests/`. Capability _semantics_ stay above: tokens are opaque payloads here. Invariants are numbered in `mia-docs/openspec/specs/components/mee-pdn/invariants.md` at the repo root (referenced by number, never name).
