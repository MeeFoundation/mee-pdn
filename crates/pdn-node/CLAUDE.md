# crates/pdn-node

The embeddable runtime core: identity / connections / data / sync services as thin glue over `data-layer`, plus the runtime's three ceremonies — pairing, linking and a cell's join.

Pairing (ADR-0011): connections are produced by establishment — `invite` mints a bearer-free payload carrying a one-time secret (no ticket, no identity proof — nothing grants durable access; the secret burns on first use), `establish` dials the pairing ALPN and runs the verify-and-burn dialogue, and the grant surface publishes, withdraws and reads grants over the connection's metadata pair — the peer's half for what the peer granted, capability and ticket together, the identity's own half for what it granted itself, capability alone (no manual recording).

Device linking (ADR-0012, the `linking` module next to `pairing`): `linking_invite` mints the same kind of bearer-free payload (one-time secret inside, nothing durable), `link` dials the linking ALPN — the inviter verifies-and-burns, registers the newcomer as pending (from the connection's authenticated peer id), and replies with fresh directory and data-namespace write tickets; `link` imports both, records its own hosting, confirms its pending record itself, and returns caught up, rolling the import back on a failure before the commit point.

Cells (the `cells` module): `create` derives the cell id from the identity's announcement key and a fresh nonce and writes the signed founding event and the creator's first device statement; `invite` mints the same kind of bearer-free payload, the secret pending for the identity and the cell; `join` dials the cell-join ALPN — the inviter verifies-and-burns, offers the sequence the newcomer's joined event takes and the device list its statement extends, writes the joined event (none for an identity already a member) and the statement into the replica of the identity the secret was minted for, and only then hands over both stores' write tickets; `join` imports them, records both tickets and the cell's directory entry, and returns once both stores have caught up and its replica folds the identity as a member. `put_record` places a record under the identity's own name at a fresh id; `append_op` adds an operation to a mergeable-document that reads here, refusing a claim and an immutable-document, and takes its operation sequence from what the replica holds under this device's author, under the runtime's state lock from that read to the write, since two appends taking one sequence share a key and the later replaces the earlier. Every record call checks the identity is a member by its replica's fold. `act` writes a membership act once the identity's role by that fold allows it, at the first sequence of the subject's chain the replica holds no entry at; a leave first flushes both stores to another member's device (`flush_cell`, waiting at most `LEAVE_FLUSH_TIMEOUT` outside the state lock), since nothing outside the departure's past leaves a departed device, then writes its left event, tombstones the cell in the directory at that sequence and forgets the record store. The cell notice consumer settles every departure data-layer reports the same way — how a kicked member's device, or a departed member's other device, learns of it — except while a join of that cell is in flight, which imports onto the tombstone before its joined event arrives, or once the directory records a later join; and for every device data-layer reports its member's statements do not list, it writes the next device statement with this device added. A member's other devices reach its cells through the connection armer's sweep (`arm_cells`): every cell the directory holds and this device does not is imported from the tickets beside its record, and the record store of every cell whose record the directory's tombstone ends is forgotten, or its tombstone opened when this device holds nothing of it; the sweep after a restart re-derives the hosted cells so. A join records, beside the cell's own tickets, the two the inviting device handed over, under the inviter's kinds, and the sweep opens a cell from both: a ticket names all its nodes as the one identity that minted it, and a sibling, or this device after a restart, has to reach the inviting device as the inviter before its replica folds anyone. The stores themselves are data-layer's cell stores.

Each `Runtime` is one running node hosting any number of identities, each added by an explicit act (create, or link from a payload); the runtime is the single owner of node assembly — the ceremonies' protocol handlers thread through `spawn` into data-layer's protocol slot — and of the hosted identities' store handles. Every operation of every service names the identity performing it and acts for that identity alone (ADR-0013): an issuer only a co-located identity holds is unknown to the caller, exactly as an issuer no identity here holds is, and an operation naming an identity this node does not host is refused as `UnknownIdentity`. Services are traits with one production implementation each; `IdentityService` derives each `PdnId` from the announcement key pair it mints first, which the identity's directory then carries to its other devices. Cross-identity access is the grant surface: a grant published over a connection's metadata pair binds the granted namespace on the audience by itself (the grant binder in `connections.rs`), and the data service's ticket share/import remains for a ticket obtained out of band — a bare ticket delivers nothing from an armed issuer, and what one identity imports without a grant is re-served to no one but the issuer's own devices. Two identities of one node meet inside the process: iroh refuses a connection to its own endpoint id, so both ceremonies' dialogues and every sync session between them run over a pipe, under the same messages and the same classification as between two nodes. `Runtime::shutdown` waits for the serving half of every ceremony in flight, over either transport (`ServingHalves`), before it stops the node: iroh's router stops every protocol side by side, so a wait inside a handler's own `shutdown` would run against a blob store already going away.

Hosting survives a restart on a directory-configured runtime (`SpawnOptions::on_directory`): each hosted identity's own subdirectory carries its hosting record — the namespace of its directory, written at the commit point of the create or link, once the store set is provisioned and armed for session classification and before the identity enters the runtime's hosted set — and `spawn` re-hosts every identity whose subdirectory holds one, through the same tail `create` runs, with the data namespace and the metadata pairs re-derived from the directory by the connection armer's sweeps, and the granted namespaces from the grant records on disk by the grant binders those sweeps start. An unreadable record stops the start; a subdirectory without one is an unfinished create or link and hosts nothing; on a memory runtime hosting ends with the process.

Scenario tests in `tests/`. No host or HTTP dependencies.
