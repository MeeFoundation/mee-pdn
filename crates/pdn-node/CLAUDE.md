# crates/pdn-node

The embeddable runtime core: identity / pods / sync services as thin glue over `data-layer`, plus the runtime's two ceremonies — linking and a pod's join — which share one framing, one kind of pending one-time secret and one unreachable-inviter error (`ceremony.rs`).

Device linking (ADR-0012, the `linking` module): `linking_invite` mints a bearer-free payload carrying a one-time secret (no ticket, no identity proof — nothing in it gives durable access; the secret burns on first use), `link` dials the linking ALPN — the inviter verifies-and-burns, registers the newcomer as pending (from the connection's authenticated peer id), and replies with a fresh PMS write ticket; `link` imports it, records its own hosting, confirms its pending record itself, and returns caught up, rolling the import back on a failure before the commit point.

Pods (the `pods` module, ADR-0015): `create` derives the pod id from the identity key and a fresh nonce and writes the signed created event and the creator's first device statement, inside a cleanup task, so a caller that drops it leaves the pod whole or nothing — the PMS's record of the pod is its commit point, and a failure before it discards both stores; `invite` mints the same kind of bearer-free payload, the secret pending for the identity and the pod; `join` dials the pod-join ALPN — the inviter verifies-and-burns, offers the sequence the newcomer's joined event takes beside the pod's created event, writes the joined event, its join statement naming the joining device — for an identity already a member, no joined event but the member's statement at version 0 naming that device, when no statement lists it — into the replica of the identity the secret was minted for, and only then hands over both stores' write tickets, each naming this device and every other device of the inviter whose address its endpoint knows (`known_addrs`), so a joiner on a node without address lookup reaches the inviter's other devices once this one is gone; the joiner reports as its own history the further of what its replica and its PMS hold of its chain, a departure reaching a sibling's PMS before its replica, and refuses a created event that does not derive the invited pod, a sequence more than one past that history and tickets to stores the created event does not name, so a modified inviter gets no signature and no record out of it; the inviter's storage failing once the secret burned is logged with the step it cut short (`JoinUnserved::Failed`), reaches a remote newcomer as the uniform refusal, and reaches a joiner on the inviter's own node as itself — no subscription reports it to the host, as `subscribe_linking_failures` does for linking; `join` records the tickets and the pod's PMS entry — the join's commit point — before it imports the stores, its own tickets minted from the handed-over ones with this device their one node, and returns once both stores have caught up and its membership view lists the identity as a member. `put_record` places a record under the identity's own name at a fresh id; `append_op` adds an operation to a mergeable-document that reads here, refusing a claim and an immutable-document, and takes its operation sequence from what the replica holds under this device's author, under the runtime's state lock from that read to the write, since two appends taking one sequence share a key and the later replaces the earlier. Every record call checks the identity is a member by its replica's membership view. `act` writes a membership act once the identity's role by that membership view allows it, at the first sequence of the subject's chain the replica holds no entry at; a leave first flushes both stores to another member's device (`flush_pod`, waiting at most `LEAVE_FLUSH_TIMEOUT` for the sessions, and beyond it for the payloads that device lacks, outside the state lock), since nothing outside the departure's past leaves a departed device, then writes its left event, tombstones the pod in the PMS at that sequence and forgets the record store, and, when the flush reached a member, waits for the tombstone's session with one the same way, so a device that stops at once is not listed as a member. Whether this device holds a pod's record store follows one rule (`holds`): the PMS's highest record of the pod decides while the device's replica of the membership store holds the identity's chain short of that record's sequence — the event the record mirrors has not arrived — and the membership view decides from there on, so a departure a later event reversed, or a join that counts for nothing, ends where the membership view does; `list` answers the pods whose record store this device holds and those the PMS holds that it has not opened. `settle_pod` applies the rule — the record store opened from the PMS's tickets, which a departure keeps, or forgotten, a departure the membership view shows tombstoned in the PMS, the tombstone opened — except while a join of that pod is in flight, which imports onto the tombstone before its joined event arrives. The pod notice consumer settles every departure and every reinstatement data-layer reports — how a removed member's device, a departed member's other device, or a device whose member's departure a later event reversed learns of it — and for every device data-layer reports its member's statements do not list, it writes the next device statement with this device added. A member's other devices reach its pods through the armer's sweep (`spawn_identity_armer` in `armer.rs`, which also confirms this device in the PMS when the confirmed set lacks it, then `arm_pods`), which settles every pod whose PMS record its holding here does not match; the sweep after a restart re-derives the hosted pods so. A join records, beside the pod's own tickets, the two the inviting device handed over, under the inviter's kinds, and the sweep opens a pod from both: a ticket names all its nodes as the one identity that minted it, and a sibling, or this device after a restart, has to reach the inviting device as the inviter before its membership view lists anyone. The stores themselves are data-layer's pod stores.

Each `Runtime` is one running node hosting any number of identities, each added by an explicit act (create, or link from a payload); the runtime is the single owner of node assembly — the ceremonies' protocol handlers thread through `spawn` into data-layer's protocol slot — and of the hosted identities' store handles. Every operation of every service names the identity performing it and acts for that identity alone (ADR-0013): a pod only a co-located identity is a member of is unknown to the caller (`UnknownPod`), exactly as a pod no identity here holds is, and an operation naming an identity this node does not host is refused as `UnknownIdentity`. Services are traits with one production implementation each; `IdentityService` derives each `PdnId` from the identity key pair it mints first, which the identity's PMS then carries to its other devices. Every path that hosts an identity — create, link, recovery — admits it through `admit_hosted` in `runtime.rs`, which enters it in the hosted set and starts its armer as one step: a hosted identity without an armer serves its PMS and never opens its pods on this device, with no error to show for it. Identities share through pods alone: an identity's PMS reaches its own devices and nobody else. Two identities of one node meet inside the process: iroh refuses a connection to its own endpoint id, so a pod's join and every sync session between them run over a pipe, under the same messages and the same classification as between two nodes. `Runtime::shutdown` waits for the serving half of every ceremony in flight, over either transport (`ServingHalves`), before it stops the node: iroh's router stops every protocol side by side, so a wait inside a handler's own `shutdown` would run against a blob store already going away.

Hosting survives a restart on a directory-configured runtime (`SpawnOptions::on_directory`): each hosted identity's own subdirectory carries its hosting record — the namespace of its PMS, written at the commit point of the create or link, once the store set is provisioned and armed for session classification and before the identity enters the runtime's hosted set — and `spawn` re-hosts every identity whose subdirectory holds one, through the same tail `create` runs, with its pods re-opened from the PMS by the armer's sweeps. An unreadable record stops the start; a subdirectory without one is an unfinished create or link and hosts nothing; on a memory runtime hosting ends with the process.

Observation (the `observation` module, `test-util` only; the contract is the observation spec of `pdn-node`): `observe_pod_for_test` merges one hosted identity's ordered subscriptions of a pod's two stores into numbered records, stamped and numbered by a task of its own that never waits on the consumer and holds no state lock, the records waiting in a buffer bounded at `RECORD_BUFFER_BOUND`; `join_pod_observed_for_test` runs `join` itself (`join_with`, which takes the import as an argument) and opens that subscription between the import and the start of either store's sync; `pod_record_listing_for_test` lists the record view entry by entry with each verdict. `PodRecord` and `ListedEntry` carry the one JSON form every host writes. The runtime's stop ends every subscription with no further record.

Scenario tests in `tests/`. No host or HTTP dependencies.
